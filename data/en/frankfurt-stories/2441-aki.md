# Aki

*1950–1996, At the Central Station*

![Exterior of the Aki Cinema in the entrance hall of the main train station, 1950.](../../images/9a7c172821778dcff63fcf86cdae590b_original.jpg)
*2025 — Author: Unbekannt/Unkown | License: Zeitung "Der Neue Deutsche Film" 1950 | Description: Exterior of the Aki Cinema in the entrance hall of the main train station, 1950.*

On 24 November 1950, the AKI news cinema – the Aktualitätenkino – opened in Frankfurt’s Main Station. One of Germany’s first 24-hour cinemas, it screened a loop program of short films. The mix of news, sports, culture and cartoons was ideal for travelers ready to spend 50 pfennigs to bridge their time waiting for a train.

*Author: Anna Rottlaender*

With its central location, round-the-clock shows and news programs, the AKI had a profile distinct from the city’s standard movie theatres. But with the rise of TV in the 1960s, the program increasingly focused on low-budget entertainment films, such as crime thrillers and later action and erotic movies.

*Author: Anna Rottländer*

In 1979, the AKI was refurbished and reopened as a modern multiplex with three screens. Except for events such as Frankfurt’s “Filmschau” film festival, it continued to primarily show sensational and erotic movies, gaining a reputation as a “shabby” station cinema until it finally closed in 1996.

*Author: Anna Rottlaender*
