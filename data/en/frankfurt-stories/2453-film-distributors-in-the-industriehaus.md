# Film distributors in the Industriehaus

*From 1924, Taunusstraße 52–60*

![Exterior facade of the film distribution company in the Industriehaus](../../images/dd7c9674f893533607e814b6f651c663_original.jpg)
*1970 — Author: unbekannt / unkown | License: Institut für Stadtgeschichte Frankfurt am Main (ISG FFM), B S7HBA, Nr. 2093 | Description: Exterior facade of the film distribution company in the Industriehaus*

“With today’s Frankfurt as Germany’s film distribution hub, home to 14 headquarters and 40 branches, the world’s cinema is represented in the city.” (Source: FAZ, 23.9.1950)

Nowadays, few remember Frankfurt’s one-time reputation as a “secret capital of film,” a status due to its major film distribution services. Even as early as the 1910s, this branch of the film business was becoming increasingly important in the city.

In the years after 1945, the Main Station neighborhood emerged as the center of film distribution in West Germany. With this development, Frankfurt took on the cinematic mantle of a divided Berlin. Nearly all the leading American and German distribution companies opened offices here, including Paramount Films, Warner Bros and Constantin Film. Initially, American distributors were entrusted with the task of teaching the West German populace democratic values. The Motion Pictures Export Association and other companies supplied the West German cinema market with newsreels and Hollywood feature films. Over the years German businesses also moved back into the neighborhood and a competition developed over attracting the largest number of movie goers. Various distributors also set up offices in the “Industriehaus” block at Taunusstraße 52–60, directly behind the Schumann Theater.

*Author: Anne Haferung*

Through acquiring and supplying films, distributors significantly influenced the programs shown on screen. For the films they wanted to show, cinema owners completed order forms – as you can see be[aa1] low. Very often, though, they could not just take popular main features, but also had to screen lesser-known works. In this way, the distributors played a major role in shaping Frankfurt’s cinema landscape. 

In the 1960s, the distributing business went into crisis. Not only were distributors competing with each other and with television, but also dealing with a glut of films in an outdated film sector changing as international structures became established. Many companies were forced to close. Today, the “Industriehaus” is a residential and commercial block with, as of 2020, 124 luxury rental apartments.

*Author: Anne Haferung*
