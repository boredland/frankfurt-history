# Luxor Cinema

*1948–1959, 10 Am Hauptbahnhof*

!["Luxor-Filmtheater" entrance, 1949](../../images/73e115f1577ec7bf60321aeff99bdc4e_original.jpg)
*1949 — Author: Erik Fenn | License: Erik Fenn, Nachlass von Friedrich Wollenberg, aufbewahrt im Deutschen Filmmuseum | Description: "Luxor-Filmtheater" entrance, 1949*

The "cinema tsar" Peter Wollenberg ran several cinemas in Frankfurt during the 1950s. In 1948 he opened the Luxor, the third Frankfurt first-run cinema after the Second World War. To do so, he had to convince the building authority and received support from the US film control office.

The cinema on the station forecourt was a classic "rubble cinema" in the truest sense of the word: Wollenberg made cooking pots out of scrapped fighter planes and traded them for building materials.

The Luxor showed West German productions as well as American films in English for the US occupying forces. It also drew attention to itself with elaborate advertising campaigns amid the ruins of the city center and a great spirit of innovation. For example, it showed the first screening of stereoscopic film (3D films) in Frankfurt.

In 1953, Peter Wollenberg changed the Luxor's program and renamed it "Studio für Filmkunst" (Studio for Film Art). It was the first Frankfurt cinema with an exclusively artistically ambitious program. However, the "art-house cinema" experiment failed in the station district: due to a lack of visitors, the Luxor had to close in 1959.

*Author: Aline Fuß*
