# Cinema "Gloria Palast"

*1927-1981, 28 Kaiserstraße*

![A long shot of the Gloria Palace in 1972.](../../images/52894e6aae1637ffe23f79c43a2d2d20_original.jpg)
*1972 — Author: Foto Dabrowksi | License: Institut für Stadtgeschichte Frankfurt am Main (ISG FFM), S7C Nr. 1998-9943, Tadeusz Dabrowski | Description: A long shot of the Gloria Palace in 1972.*

In late summer 1927, the Gloria Palast opened its doors to the public. The new cinema’s inaugural feature was the silent movie The Master of Nuremberg. Located in a furniture factory’s former office building, the lavishly decorated first-run cinema offered seating for audiences of around 700. In 1928, one year after opening, the Gloria Palast showcased a technical revolution. Long before the talkies became standard in 1930s, this was Frankfurt’s first cinema to screen two sound films in a special performance.

*Author: Rosa Krohn*

After the Nazi Party came to power in 1933, prestigious cinemas such as the Gloria Palast became venues for the regime to celebrate the premières of its propaganda films. In 1933, the screening of the film Storm Trooper Brand by Franz Seitz Senior caused a scandal reported in the Frankfurter Nachrichten newspaper.

*Author: Rosa Krohn*

For several years after the war, the building was used by the US military. The Gloria Palast reopened in the 1950s. Now with elegant blueish-beige wallpaper and an elevator to the balcony floor, it once again offered – just as before the war – the pleasures of cinema in a more sophisticated ambience for the well-situated middle classes. In the 1960s, the cinema was again extensively renovated, this time even with a smoking lounge separated from the auditorium by a glass panel. Over the years, the number of seats were reduced, but films were still shown here into the 1980s. In 1981, the cinema opened its doors for the last time for a screening of The Last Days of Pompeii, an aptly titled film for this final performance.

*Author: Rosa Krohn*
