# New Panorama at main train station Frankfurt

*1894–1904/1905, corner of Niddastraße and Bahnstraße*

![View on the entrance of the New Panorama.](../../images/03426210ba747f2b1fcb94bdf22b1dff_original.jpg)
*1903 — Author: Gottlieb Vömel | License: Institut für Stadtgeschichte Frankfurt am Main (ISG FFM), S7Vö Nr. 1639 | Description: View on the entrance of the New Panorama.*

On 4 February 1894, the Frankfurter Zeitung newspaper wrote of: “A new[,] outstanding attraction in our city[,] certain to enjoy the great acclaim and popularity it deserves.” 
 
This attraction was the New Panorama, designed as a successor to earlier Frankfurt panoramas in the nineteenth-century. [Link POI Electrotechnical Exhibition] From then on, it was on show in the building with a strikingly domed roof to the north-west of the Main Station.
 
Inside the New Panorama, visitors stood on a viewing platform with an immersive 360° view of a vast circular painting. Such displays were often enhanced by realistic foreground elements. Most likely, Frankfurt’s New Panorama also added appropriate features and requisites at floor level between the circular paintings and the central viewing platform to make the panoramic scenes even more realistic. Depending on the scene, these additions might include objects such as sand, stone or parts of suits of armor. 
 
These 360° paintings frequently portrayed historical battles. For the opening in 1894, Professor Loius Braun, one of the leading panorama painters of his day, created a massive circular painting of the Battle of Lützen (1632).

*Author: René Hanauer*

In 1897, a newspaper reported the portrayal of the naval Battle of Trafalgar (1805) was so vibrantly realistic visitors could almost see the “flashes and smoke” and hear the “splintering of masts and rigging”.
 
Yet by 1905, after six exhibitions, the panorama’s golden age was already over. Just as in other places, public interest was already dwindling as the panorama was edged out by a new medium – cinema.

*Author: René Hanauer*
