# Schumann-Theater

*1905–1944, 16 Am Hauptbahnhof*

![Colored postcard of the Schumann-Theater.](../../images/2986317f271cac3b0b03cd7c2d3275be_original.jpg)
*Author: unbekannt / unkown | License: Institut für Stadtgeschichte Frankfurt am Main (ISG FFM), S17 Nr. 402, 3 Postkarten o. lfd. Nr. | Description: Colored postcard of the Schumann-Theater.*

Germany’s largest cinema

When the Albert Schumann Theater was reopened in 1923, sociologist and film critic Siegfried Kracauer wrote: “The Schumann Variety Theatre is dead – long live the Schumann Picture Palace!” 
In 1905, circus director Albert Schumann had commissioned a lavish art nouveau theatre capable of holding audiences of up to 5,000. From the very start, film was part of the entertainment program. After reopening in 1923, for a few months the theater was Germany's largest picture palace. It even arranged major stage productions with personal guest appearances by glamorous silent film stars, including Asta Nielsen and Henny Porten – a policy setting the Schumann Theater off from the city’s other cinemas. The evening’s entertainment may have usually ended with a film, but cinema never completely replaced the varied stage program comprising a mix of cinema, variety theatre, operetta, circus acts, dance and drama.

*Author: Anke Gross*

The Schumann Theater

Originally, Albert Schumann was looking for a permanent site for his circus. The theater’s basement floor included stables for 150 horses as well as cages for lions. Later, boxing and wrestling bouts replaced the animal shows. Under the Nazi regime, the Schumann Theater remained in operation. Its program became increasingly nationalist, and the building itself also served as a location for children joining the Hitler Youth and the League of German Girls (BDM). During the war, the long tunnel-like underground beer bar served as an air-raid shelter. In 1944, an air raid badly damaged the building, ending the theater’s activities forever. After the war, the theater became a leisure facility for the US Army. In 1966, it was finally demolished and the site rebuilt with a parking garage and office block.
