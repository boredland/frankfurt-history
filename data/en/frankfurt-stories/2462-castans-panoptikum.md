# Castan's Panoptikum

*1879–1910,67–69 Kaiserstraße*

![Photograph of the building of Castan's Panoptikum and Castan's Kinematograph in Frankfurt, around 1907](../../images/6af257f860544cdefca03476d6baee9a_original.jpg)
*ca. 1907 — Author: unbekannt/unkown | License: Institut für Stadtgeschichte Frankfurt am Main (ISG FFM), S7A 1998/11.824 | Description: Photograph of the building of Castan's Panoptikum and Castan's Kinematograph in Frankfurt, around 1907*

“To a large extent, the display does justice to the desire for entertainment, though equally to educational and scientific needs [...] so that a visit to the Panopticon is one of the most interesting pleasures that Frankfurt has to offer for every class and every age.” - Foreword to the 1904 exhibition guide

In 1879, Gustav and Louis Castan opened the Frankfurt branch of Castan's Panopticon – a multimedia museum of wonder. The Castan brothers, an anatomist and a sculptor, founded Germany’s first waxworks museum in Berlin in 1869. When they opened the Frankfurt branch ten years later, they followed the same concept and design. With more branches launched in other cities in Germany and across Europe, the Frankfurt branch was part of a pan-European media empire.

In a series of themed sections, the exhibition showed objects from the collection, including around 450 life-size wax figures of major historical or contemporary personalities as well as local figures from Frankfurt. In addition, there were over 220 ethnological artefacts from Africa, Asia and the South Pacific, torture implements, anatomical models and an international shoe collection. The red “Hall for Scientific Demonstrations” presented live shows with “living” performers.

Although the Panopticon aspired to a mix of education and entertainment, it often merely reproduced the content and portrayals of colonial ideas of the world and power relations – without questioning them.

The cinematograph in Castan's Panopticon

Around 1900, the Panopticon could boast its latest sensation – a cinematograph! Now, American short films flickered across the screen between the wax figures. In 1904, the Frankfurt branch was taken over by a company from Brussels. Three years later, it set up a cinema auditorium in the red event hall with seating for 120 people. As the “Cinematograph Castan's Panopticon” this became one of the first cinemas in Frankfurt. Julius Wiesbader, already the co-founder of the two other Cinematograph Theaters in Kaiserstraße, ran the business until it closed in 1910.

Afterwards, the city of Frankfurt took over around 300 anatomical models for a planned museum of health. The models were to serve as teaching aids in explaining tuberculosis, alcoholism and sexually transmitted diseases. Eighty-six ethnological artefacts were sold to the Museum of Ethnology, founded in 1904. Today as the Museum of World Cultures, one of its tasks is critically re-examining this legacy and researching into the provenance of these artefacts.

*Author: Svetlana Svyatskaya*
