# Kinematographentheater/ Welt-Kino / Elite-Lichtspiele/ Corso-Lichtspiele

*1910–1913, 64 Kaiserstraße*

Frankfurt’s first cinema – the Kinematographentheater

Later: Welt-Kino / Elite-Lichtspiele / Corso-Lichtspiele

Frankfurt’s first cinema was located at Kaiserstraße 60. Called the Kinematographentheater – the Cinematography Theater –, it opened its doors here on 3 March 1906. That same day, the Kleine Presse newspaper reported:
 
“At the opening ceremony, a man involved in the enterprise explained that cinematography has an element of serving ‘the public good’, as it also provides a way for those less well-off to see distant lands and peoples at little cost.”

Most likely, this was a quote from the cinema’s owner August Haslwanter. A proprietor from Hamburg, Haslwanter was quick to recognize cinema’s economic potential, and he became one of the first owners of a cinema chain in Germany. He had rented these rooms in Kaiserstraße from Julius Wiesbader and Max Bauer, co-founders of PAGU and themselves planning to open a cinema.

The changing weekly program included ten to twelve short documentaries of topical events as well as short feature films. The screenings were often accompanied by live music or narrators (sometimes called ‘explainers’). According to the Frankfurter Nachrichten newspaper, this made it “a pleasure for everyone [...] to let the flickering images roll past them.”

From 1907 on two evenings a week, the cinema also showed “by popular request,” as noted by the Der Kinematographmagazine, “the latest saucy Paris novelties” for a purely male audience.

In 1916, Karoline Hoffmann and Bernhard Löwenstein took over the cinema, renaming it the Welt-Kino. Later, the name changed again to the Elite-Lichtspiele. From 1933, after the Nazi regime forced the cinema to transfer to “Aryan” owners, it became the Corso-Lichtspiele. In its heyday in 1926, the cinema had seating for an audience of around 500.
