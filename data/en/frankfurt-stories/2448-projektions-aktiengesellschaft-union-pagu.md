# Projektions-Aktiengesellschaft „Union" (PAGU)

*1910–1913, 64 Kaiserstraße*

![](../../images/39cbf707691137ec9de669f7177fe821_original.jpg)
*1932 — License: LichtBildBühne_24.Mai1932_25Jahrgang, Nr. 119*

“The Union became the center of the world, the film world.”
  P. Davidson, quoted from Lothar Stark in Lichtbild-Bühne, 1932

Frankfurt’s cinematic history tells of courageous ideas and far-sighted entrepreneurs. Over the years, the city became the most important driver in motion picture art and the film business in Germany. From 1909 to 1913, Kaiserstraße 64 was the headquarters of the PAGU, the Projection Joint Stock Company “Union,” Germany’s first shareholder company for film. The PAGU emerged from the AKTG, the General Cinematography Theater Company, founded in Frankfurt in 1906.

In 1932, film producer Lothar Stark described the company’s very modest start in those early days: “The Union ‘office’ was just two rooms. One door announced ‘Director’ in gold letters on a black background, the other was the ‘Office'. [...] a narrow office area was separated by a little wooden barrier, and a cardboard sign above the yellow bench there said, ‘Waiting Room’.”

This was where Frankfurt entrepreneur Paul Davidson revolutionized the cinema business. Davidson was the first to bring together production, distribution, technology and his own cinemas under one roof, laying the foundation for the modern film business. The PAGU already operated several Union Theaters in Frankfurt. From here, it expanded throughout Germany and beyond, with ten Union Theaters (U.T.) cinemas in Berlin alone, as well as others in Brussels, Amsterdam and Strasbourg.

In 1911, Frankfurt made cinematic history. In the PAGU offices, Paul Davidson signed a contract with both the Danish star Asta Nielsen, famous for her film Afgrunden (The Abyss, 1910), and her director Urban Gad. This agreement was a sensation – they were contracted for 30 films in just three years. The films were distributed to the cinemas as a series with exclusive screening rights. With demand for them solely driven by Asta Nielsen’s popularity, she became the first international movie star.

Not only did she play an active role in shaping her public image, but also secured a fair financial return from the success of her acting. This dovetailing of personality cult, performance art and marketing became the basis for the star system still associated with Hollywood today.

In 1913, Davidson moved his company headquarters to Berlin. There, the PAGU engaged such major figures in theatre as Max Reinhardt and the young Ernst Lubitsch. Despite the financial crisis in 1915 and the sale of the Union cinemas to the Danish Nordisk Film company, Davidson could revive his career as a successful producer – thanks to Lubitsch’s films. Movies such as The Eyes Of The Mummy (1918) starring Pola Negri became box-office hits, and PAGU productions including The Golem: How He Came Into The World (1920) made film history.

Although the PAGU became part of the newly-founded Ufa, the Universum Film AG, in 1917 – the new company’s roots were here in Frankfurt. As economist Karl Zimmerschied noted in 1922 in his early account of the rise of the German film industry, Frankfurt was the real “birthplace of the Ufa.”

For more on the PAGU films, see the overview on filmportal.de.

Today, Frankfurt’s Kinothek Asta Nielsen commemorates Nielsen and the importance of women in cinema. The kinothek-asta-nielsen.de webpage has more details on the archive and program (in German).
