# Hansa Lichtspiele

*1907–1993, 50 Kaiserstraße*

![The "Hansa Lichtspiele" in 1981](../../images/6c06d70e61a4a0e93b7d55b2aeabe304_original.jpg)
*1981 — Author: unbekannt / unkown | License: Institut für Stadtgeschichte | Description: The "Hansa Lichtspiele" in 1981*

The Kinephontheater cinema opened on 15 November 1907. It primarily screened “sound pictures,” each lasting three to four minutes. This early form of sound film used a sound-on-disc system to accompany the films with music played by a gramophone. The “Kinephon” part of the movie house’s name is an allusion to this special technical process. The films included comic pieces, but above all opera arias and songs. The sound and film were recorded separately, rather like the playback technique in modern music videos.

*Author: Hauke Henkel*

In 1908, with the operators accused of poor management, the “sound picture” entrepreneur Heinrich Putzo took over the cinema, renaming it the Boulevard Theater. But as prices for “sound picture” shows collapsed, the cinema’s profits plummeted. By 1911, Putzo was bankrupt and forced to sell the cinema.

It then changed owners and names several times. In 1921, it appeared as the Hansa-Lichtspiele for the first time in a city of Frankfurt address book. With the building left largely unscathed by the Second World War, the cinema could already open its doors again as early as 19 October 1945.

*Author: Hauke Henkel*

In 1963, a fire broke out in the cellar which left the auditorium slightly damaged. The screenings of the western Six Black Horses (USA, 1962) had to be cancelled.

From 1977, the Hansa Lichtspiele was managed by Brummer & Schweikart. Munich producer Alois Brummer and cinema operator Hans-Günter Schweikart refurbished the Hansa with three small screens and a program of action and sex films. The cinema finally closed in 1993.

*Author: Hauke Henkel*
