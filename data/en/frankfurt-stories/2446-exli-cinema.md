# Exli cinema

*1910–1979, 7 Hohenzollernstraße*

![](../../images/96f5dfd79261db0f348e0027fb7e1737_original.jpg)

The cinema in Düsseldorfer Straße, originally Hohenzollernstraße 7, had a long and checkered history going back over 70 years. When it was founded in 1910 as the Hohenzollern Theater, this was Frankfurt’s first and only free-standing cinema. But just four years later, it had to vacate the site for the newly constructed Hotel Excelsior. In 1916, it then re-opened on the hotel’s premises. Now called the National Theater, it was reborn as a lavish picture palace with an orchestra pit. The cinema ran into financial problems as movies transitioned to sound. In 1932, the Hotel Excelsior took over the management of the cinema, renaming it the Exli. The US military commandeered the cinema in 1945. Nine years later, after renovation, the Exli opened again. It remained in the same form until it finally closed in 1978.

*Author: Fran Evers*

A particularly interesting aspect of this history is the attempt to respond to the growing popularity of the talkies in the early 1930s. Hopes were placed in the model of the renowned Berlin Kamera, an influential repertory cinema attracting such figures as G. W. Pabst. The Kamera was managed by the “Society for good cinema” (Gesellschaft für den guten Film[aa1] ) which, in 1931, also took over the Exli re-opening it as the Kurbel. But the planned funding through the Degeto, formed by the Society, never materialized. As a result, the Kurbel had to close again in March 1932, after just five months as Frankfurt’s first art house cinema.
